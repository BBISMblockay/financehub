How credit is assigned

  1. Start with Shopify’s actual sales activity, including reversals.
  2. Look at each order’s captured Shopify journey.
  3. Give last-click credit to the latest identifiable non-direct visit within the selected window. A later direct visit does not erase it.
  4. Keep missing evidence Unattributed.
  5. Check both net and total sales against independent Shopify totals for every day.

Starts counts orders whose earliest identifiable non-direct touch in the window was this channel; it does not prove first-ever discovery. Assists counts orders where the channel appeared between the start and the close. Closes counts orders it got last-click credit for. A one-channel journey is both a start and a close.

Empty, unavailable or pending journeys are not Direct. Raw order landing fields may supply a labeled first-touch fallback; they cannot replace a later known visit. AfterSell orders require a verified parent link to inherit another order’s credit. A 60-day window does not guarantee complete tracking history. Order counts include older orders with reversals during the reporting period.

Attribution describes observed evidence, not causality or incremental sales.

Where the data comes from

Dedicated ingestion captures Shopify order/day sales, daily controls, paginated journeys and allowlisted landing fields. Same-company Silo campaign/ad records help identify campaigns. Spend comes from the ad-platform and Redo syncs.

Chronological observed visits → purchase. Times use the Shopify store timezone. Highlighted touches explain the selected window’s allocation.
