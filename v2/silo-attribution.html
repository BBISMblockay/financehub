Model & limitations

How we got this answer

  1. Start with Shopify’s actual sales activity, including reversals.
  2. Look at each order’s captured Shopify journey.
  3. Credit the latest identifiable non-direct visit within the selected window once. A later direct visit does not erase it.
  4. Keep missing evidence Unattributed.
  5. Check both net and total sales against independent Shopify totals for every day.

Introduced by means the earliest identifiable non-direct touch we observed within the window. It does not prove first-ever brand discovery. Assisted by lists other observed channels before purchase; counts overlap.

Meta → Redo email → Direct → Purchase can credit Redo while preserving Meta’s role. Commerce channels remain distinct from advertising claims. Order counts include older orders with reversals during the reporting period.

Empty, unavailable or pending journeys are not Direct. Raw order landing fields may supply a labeled first-touch fallback; they cannot replace a later known visit. AfterSell orders require a verified parent link to inherit another order’s credit. A 60-day window does not guarantee complete tracking history.

Revenue reconciliation proves dollars were counted once. Attribution describes observed evidence, not causality or incremental sales. Spend and ROAS are outside v1.

Where the data comes from

Dedicated ingestion captures Shopify order/day sales, daily controls, paginated journeys and allowlisted landing fields. Same-company Silo campaign/ad records help identify campaigns. This production page reads saved snapshots; the earlier pilot required additional live Shopify reads.

Chronological observed visits → purchase. Times use the Shopify store timezone. Highlighted touches explain the selected window’s allocation.
